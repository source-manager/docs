    A tour of the window¶

    The window is one shell with a set of destinations, and the frame around them is two strips where each strip is one kind of thing.

    ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ ⌂ › my-project › main ↑2 ↓3            Fetch ▾  Pull  Push ▾  Actions ▾  ⚙ │  the workspace bar
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│  Changes 3   History   Rebase   Conflicts   Blame   Manage   …       │  the tab bar
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│  Rebasing — stopped on conflicts in 2 files    Conflicts  Abort      │  the operation banner
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│                                                                      │
│                          one surface                                 │
│                                                                      │
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│  9 repositories · 2 with work in progress                            │  the status bar
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

    The workspace bar¶

    Says which repository, which branch, and what can be done to that pair.

    Home › my-project › main is a breadcrumb rather than a sidebar, and each crumb is the way to change what it states:

    • Home goes to the fleet.
    • The repository crumb opens the fleet as a filtered list, with the marker dot, the drift counts and the group as a tag.
    • The branch crumb opens the branches with a filter, carries ↑ahead ↓behind, and is the only place that says which branch those counts are about. Choosing one narrows the history to it and moves nothing.

    Then Fetch (with a caret for fetch-and-prune), Pull, Push (with a caret for the two forcing verbs), the actions menu, and the way into the settings.

    Push always means push. It refuses a non-fast-forward in its own words rather than replacing itself with a forcing verb at the moment forcing is dangerous; both ways past that refusal are behind the caret, and the plain force needs a third gesture. See Remotes and transport.

    On the fleet page the transport verbs and the crumbs are hidden — the fleet is not one repository and stopped pretending to be — and the foot says how many repositories are registered and how many have work in progress.

    The tab bar¶

    The tab bar is the list of destinations. Nine of them have a tab:

    Tab What it holds
    Changes The working tree, staged and unstaged, with a diff
    History The graph, the search, and a commit's contents
    Rebase Plan a rebase, edit the plan, then apply it
    Conflicts Three-way resolution, per hunk
    Blame Per-line blame with a revision list
    Manage Stashes, worktrees, submodules, subtrees, branches, remotes, hooks, LFS, sparse, actions, the operations log
    Pull requests The forge's proposals, and a review
    Checks CI: this commit's checks, and the forge's own runs
    Metrics What the history and the forge say, over a period

    Changes and Conflicts carry a count of what they hold. Zero renders as nothing at all — "Conflicts 0" is what the tab would read on all but a handful of days, and a number that is almost always the same stops being read, while a badge appearing where there was none is the whole signal.

    Five more destinations have no tab and are reached by name from the palette or from an object: Fleet (the Home crumb), Compare, Settings, File history and Reflog. A tenth tab would cost every other tab some of its width on every surface.

    Exactly one surface exists at a time

    Surfaces are loaded on demand rather than stacked. History and Blame each hold thousands of rows, and there is no reason for the ones you are not looking at to exist. The consequence you can see: a filter typed into a search box is gone when you come back, while a reading habit — split diff, wrapped lines, tree view — is remembered, because those live in the settings rather than on the surface.

    The operation banner¶

    The design rule the whole app is arranged around is that operations are modal in time, not in space. A running or stopped operation shows in a banner under the header on every surface, so walking away from a half-finished rebase is safe and it cannot be lost track of.

    It reports five kinds of state — a stopped rebase, merge or cherry-pick, a bisect, a detached HEAD — plus two rows of its own for a transfer in flight and a long write over many paths. The way out is always on it, and never gated: a lapsed licence cannot leave anybody in a detached HEAD with no button.

    Operations are entered from the object¶

    There is no menu of verbs anywhere. Rebase is entered from a commit in History; revert from a commit; bisect from the commit where the thing still worked; ignore from the file in Changes; a proposal from a branch row or from the comparison page. Conflicts opens by itself when something stops on one.

    That is why a few features have no obvious front door, and why Bisect, stacked branches and workflow recipes each get a walkthrough of their own.

    Panels are dragged, and stay where you put them¶

    Every two-panel division goes through one restyled splitter: the handle is in the app's hairline idiom, and the size survives a relaunch and being restored into a window smaller than the one it was chosen in. Sizes are in pixels rather than fractions, because a quarter of a wide window is not readable as a quarter of a small one.

    Three divisions deliberately have no handle: the three conflict columns (equal thirds are what makes three readings comparable), the blame gutter (a per-row column is not a division), and the two Rebase steps (a form has a right width rather than a preferred one — dragging prose narrow is the same words in more lines).
