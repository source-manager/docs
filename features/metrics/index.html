    Repository metrics¶

    Licensing — Paid. This page is metrics. What a licence adds

    What the history and the forge say about a repository, over a period you choose. No desktop client in the comparison has anything like it; this is normally a separate SaaS product.

    Four tabs, and the order is an argument¶

    History, People, Flow, CI — and History is the tab that opens.

    Every tool in this space that earned a bad reputation earned it by making a per-developer table its front page. Grouping this page into tabs was the chance to promote a leaderboard to a landing page, and it was not taken: everything that names a person is behind the second tab under the sentence about what a line count measures, and the ranked list is still last inside it. A test requires that the tab which opens draws no card naming a person.

    The groups are cut by what a figure is about rather than by where it came from, which is why two of them mix the commit graph and the forge: "You, in this history" and "You, against the rest" are one question asked of two sources, and filing them apart to keep the sources tidy would be tidying the wrong thing.

    The window control stays above the tabs, because the period bounds every group and a window that jumped between tabs would make four readings out of one.

    The window is two shapes, and they are not sugar for one another¶

    A preset is "the last N days" and slides with the clock.

    A custom period is two dates and does not. Somebody who picks 1 January to 31 March and comes back in June is asking about the first quarter, not about "the ninety days ending ninety days ago" — so the pair is stored as it was chosen and reopens meaning what it meant. Storing it as offsets would have kept one representation and moved the period every night, which is the sort of thing you discover by disbelieving a number you were right about last week.

    The far end is inclusive of the whole day: a period naming its last day and then excluding it is the off-by-one nobody would think to check.

    The period bounds both halves. The page used to carry a sentence saying its two halves covered different spans — a caption apologising for the one fault a single window control exists to prevent. Two things about the far end are load-bearing:

    • A commit newer than it is skipped and never a reason to stop. The walk is newest-first, so a period in the past is reached only by crossing everything above it, and treating those as "out of window" would end the walk on its first commit and report an empty repository.
    • Crossing thousands of commits to reach a period is the state a batched walk can look hung in, so the count of what was passed over is published and the reading state says it — and if the walk gives up at its limit before arriving, that count is the whole explanation rather than "no commits in this window", which would be reporting an absence of knowledge as a measurement.

    Dates are typed or picked. The calendar's day cells register no keyboard entry — forty-two entries reading "17" would spend the alphabet twice over on a target that is not nameable without its month — and the keyboard route to a date is the field beside the grid.

    From the history¶

    Commit size, cadence, where change lands, how thinly knowledge is spread, and who did it.

    Counting lines means diffing every commit against its parent, which makes it the most expensive read in the app — so the walk is batched against a time budget, is cancellable, stops at a limit and says so, and is outside the general refresh.

    Figures are published once, when the walk ends. A median over the first tenth of a walk is not "the median so far", it is a different number that moves under you.

    Two things are counted the way they are because the alternative is how a line count usually lies:

    • A merge is counted and never diffed, so nobody is credited with the branch they merged.
    • Renames are exact-match-only, so a directory move is not two hundred lines of work.

    The mean sits beside a median and a p90, which is also the honest answer to excluding generated files, since nothing in a repository reliably declares them.

    From the forge¶

    Sizes and review timings cost one request per pull request, so they sit behind a button that prints its price before it is pressed. Time open, time to merge, throughput and the revert-based derivation are free off the list already fetched.

    Time open is a distribution rather than a mean, because "12 open" and "12 open, oldest 40 days" are different situations.

    First review to merge is labelled a wall clock — it holds waiting, further rounds and CI — because calling it "time to address comments" would claim to measure typing.

    Opening the page is not a request, and the button means "spend again". A surface is loaded on demand, so nothing it held used to survive walking to History and back; both answers the free half stands on are keyed by the question now, and what is served from disk arrives labelled — "stored, read 14h ago" — because a page of medians is where a stored answer wearing a live one's face would cost the most: nobody re-derives a median in their head to check it.

    The read time reported is the oldest of the answers in force, since a figure standing on a list read a minute ago and a run list read yesterday is as old as the older of them.

    DORA¶

    No figure exists without the definition that produced it. The tile cannot render a number without the sentence, and the definition is computed beside the arithmetic rather than composed by the surface: "lead time: 3.2 days" with nothing saying what counted as a deployment is the number people take into a review meeting and get wrong.

    The headline definition is a CI deploy run — a completed run whose name, file or job matches a pattern you set, on the shipping branch — read through a provider-neutral seam, with merges, tags and the forge's own deployments endpoint as fallbacks for a repository whose CI this app cannot see.

    Nothing matching is a state rather than a zero: the page names the pattern it looked for and the runs that actually happened.

    CI health¶

    Six tiles and a per-workflow table, moved here off the Checks surface whole. Those tiles answered a different question from the one that surface is opened with: "is anything broken right now" is a glance at a list, and a success rate over the last hundred runs is something to study — and studying figures is what this page is for. Checks carries a button here rather than a copy, because two drawings of one set of tiles is two chances for one of them to stop agreeing with the list beneath it.

    Two things it publishes are worth knowing:

    • The success rate's denominator is the runs that reached a verdict, and the tile names it: a cancelled build is not a failure and a skipped one is not a pass, and dividing by all of them would report a repository that cancels a lot as one that fails a lot.
    • A rate over nothing decided is absent, not zero. "0% passed" and "nothing has finished" are opposite pieces of news, and drawing the second as the first is how a CI page first loses somebody's trust.

    The span the figures cover comes back with them, because a rate over what this app fetched is not a rate over the repository and only the arithmetic knows which.

    The figures are provider-neutral by construction: they read only the fields the provider seam documents, which is what keeps a tile from quietly disappearing the day a second provider arrives.

    You, against the rest¶

    The four things asked for here were built, and nothing that argued against them was dropped.

    The project's position — that a per-developer leaderboard is how tools in this space earn their reputations, and that per-author review latency beside a name reads as a judgement on the one participant with no influence over their queue — is still true, is still printed, and is now printed beside the figures rather than instead of them. The honest response to being asked for it anyway, with that understood, was to compute it correctly rather than badly or not at all.

    Four decisions are the design:

    • Two latencies, never one. The time somebody took to give a review is about them; the time their own proposals waited is about the queue they were in. They are separate measures under separate names, and the table's last column says which of the two it is. Folding them together would name the wrong person for one of them.
    • An absent figure is absent. Every median travels with the count it was taken over, a row without the figure is not in the denominator, and a ranking drops what it cannot compute rather than ranking it last — last place is a claim about somebody and "this was never fetched for you" is not one. Counting a person the sample never reached as a zero would drag the team median towards a number nobody had, which is the most flattering mistake available here.
    • You are inside your own distribution. Excluding yourself would make "the team median" a different population for every reader, and two people comparing notes would disagree about a number they had both read off the same page.
    • Every measure carries the definition that produced it. "Reviews given: 4" is a number about a person; "four of somebody else's proposals had at least one submitted review from them, over the 30 sampled" is a number about the data.

    "The team" is every login that authored a proposal or submitted a review in what this app fetched, inside the window — not the repository's collaborators, which nothing here asks for.

    The three cards sit at the bottom in increasing order of how easily they are misread: the table of everybody, then you against the distribution, then the ranked list dead last, drawn plainly — no medals, no colour, no "1st" — under a control naming what it ranked by. rank 3 of 9 is a fact about a list; a badge would be a claim about a person.

    It costs a request, so it is off by default. A page that spent a request because somebody opened a tab is a page people learn to avoid.

    Identity¶

    Authors are grouped by lowercased email with every name seen kept beside — one person with three addresses is three rows, and the page says so above the table rather than in a comment, because a half-built identity map merges some duplicates and leaves you believing the rest are real.

    A forge login and a commit address are two identities and this app maps neither onto the other. Guessing would merge some people and leave you believing the rest were real — the same argument. So each half says who it is about, in its own vocabulary: the forge half is about a login, and the history half is about this checkout's own user.email, which is the address git will write on the next commit made here. An address with nothing in the window is a state and not a zero.

    Which figures are computed¶

    The two halves of this page cost wildly different things, and the chooser behind the page's own Figures button is arranged around the costs rather than around the cards: a group is a price.

    Group Costs
    Line counts A patch per changed file per commit
    Per-path tallies The tallies off the same diff — with both off, no tree-to-tree diff is taken at all, which is the whole saving
    DORA One request per read for the CI runs
    Proposal sizes Two per pull request, behind the button that already prints its cost
    CI health One request, under its own key
    Cadence, authors, flow, people Free, and the chooser says so in as many words

    That honesty is load-bearing: switching the per-path tallies off on their own saves a hash entry per path and nothing else, because the paths come off the diff the line counts already paid for.

    A group being off must not be a hidden card over figures that were computed anyway, so the walk really skips the work, the fetch really returns before its requests, and the Measure button really leaves the palette and the hint keys. The enabled set is part of the walk's own result key, because a walk that skipped the patches answered a different question — without that, switching the line counts back on would be served the cheap walk's answer, a page of zeros that "Read again" could not fix either.

    The choice is stored as the off list, because an enabled-list cannot tell "nobody has chosen" from "everything is switched off" — and a group added by a later build would be hidden by an enabled-list written before it existed. Every group that already drew something defaults to on.

    The ticks live where the price is legible, which is this page; the settings page carries only the reset and a sentence.
